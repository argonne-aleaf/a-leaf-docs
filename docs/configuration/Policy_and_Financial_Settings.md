# Policy and Financial Settings

This page explains how to represent policy and technology-cost assumptions in an A-LEAF study. Policy settings cover tax credits, renewable and clean-energy standards, and carbon targets and prices. Financial settings cover how technology costs are populated from cost datasets.

These settings act mainly on expansion planning, but they also affect operation, reliability assessment, and linked workflows.

The policy instruments covered are:

- `ITC` (investment tax credit)
- `PTC` (production tax credit)
- `CTAX` (carbon tax)
- `RPS` (renewable portfolio standard)
- `CEGT` (clean energy generation target)
- `CERT` (carbon emission reduction target)

The financial tables covered are `ATB Setting` and `Storage Cost and Performance`.

## 1. Policy settings

### Where policy inputs come from

A policy is defined by three inputs that work together:

1. Case-level switches, targets, and penalties in the `Simulation Configuration` sheet (see [Simulation Configuration Reference](./Simulation_Configuration_Reference.md#12-policy-and-regulation)).
2. Policy geography from the network configuration workbook: `policy_zone_boundary_type` and `policy_data_resolution_type` define the zones in which RPS, clean-energy, and carbon targets are evaluated (see [Network Configuration Reference](../database/Network_Configuration_Reference.md)).
3. Technology eligibility in the `Gen Technology` sheet, together with the year-by-year value tables `ITC` and `PTC`.

Targets are set as one global value per case. A global target applies to every policy zone; it overrides any zone-specific targets in the network configuration rather than combining with them.

### `ITC` and `PTC`

The `ITC` and `PTC` sheets give year-by-year credit values by technology group (`UNITGROUP`).

**Switching them on.** Two conditions must both hold:

- the case-level flag is on: `ITC_Flag` or `PTC_Flag` in `Simulation Configuration`;
- the technology is eligible: `ITC Flag` or `PTC Flag` is `TRUE` on its `Gen Technology` row.

The case flag turns the credit on for the case; the technology flag decides which technologies receive it.

**Investment tax credit.** The credit reduces the capital cost of a new investment: the effective capital cost is the technology capital cost multiplied by `(1 - itc)`, where `itc` is the credit fraction for that technology and year. The lower capital cost makes eligible technologies more attractive in the expansion model and lowers the reported investment cost.

**Production tax credit.** The credit is paid per unit of energy generated by eligible technologies for a limited period and is included in system-cost accounting as a reduction of cost:

| Asset | Credit period |
|---|---|
| New investments | 10 years from the investment year, limited to the years remaining in the study horizon. The credit rate is fixed at the value for the investment year. |
| Existing non-nuclear assets | The first 5 years of the study horizon. |
| Existing nuclear assets | Every year of the study horizon. |

Only technologies with `PTC Flag` set to `TRUE` receive the credit. Credits are discounted with the study discount rate.

!!! note "Supported years"
    Credit values are read for the years 2025 to 2050. A study year before 2025 uses the 2025 value, and a study year after 2050 uses the 2050 value. Every eligible technology needs a value in the `ITC` or `PTC` sheet for the years it uses; a missing value stops the run with an error.

**What changes in the results.** With credits on, expect more investment in eligible technologies, lower reported capital or production cost for them, and, through changed dispatch economics, different capacity mixes and emissions. Compare against a case with the flags off to isolate the effect.

### `CTAX`

`CTAX` is a carbon price applied to emissions from generation. It adds an emission-proportional cost to each unit's dispatch cost, so it raises the running cost of high-emission units and shifts dispatch and investment toward lower-emission resources. The tax is expressed per metric tonne of CO2, consistent with the `Emission_CO2` rates. A value of `0` applies no tax. The carbon tax is reported as a separate cost item in the system-cost outputs.

### `RPS`

The renewable portfolio standard requires a minimum share of energy from eligible renewable resources within each policy zone.

**Switching it on.** In `Simulation Configuration`:

- `RPS_Flag` enables the requirement.
- `RPS_Global_Target_Value` sets the target as a fraction of energy (for example `0.5` for 50 percent).
- `Allow_Alternative_RPS_Compliance_Flag` makes the requirement soft. When it is on, the model may fall short of the target and pays `RPS_Penalty` (price per MWh of shortfall) instead; when it is off, the target is a hard requirement and the case is infeasible if it cannot be met.

**Geography.** The target is evaluated per policy zone, so the same global value is enforced at different regional levels depending on `policy_zone_boundary_type` and `policy_data_resolution_type` in the network configuration.

**What changes in the results.** Renewable investment and generation rise in zones where the target binds. With soft compliance, the shortfall and its penalty cost appear in the cost outputs (the `RPS_Penalty` and `RPS_Penalty_CF` cost columns and the `RPS_Target_Slack` output); a large penalty cost indicates the target could not be met economically.

### `CEGT`

The clean energy generation target requires a minimum share of generation from clean resources, again per policy zone.

**Switching it on.** In `Simulation Configuration`:

| Setting | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Target_EXP_Flag` | Enforces the target in the expansion model. |
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Target_EXP_Type` | `Annual`: the target must hold over the full year. `Daygroup`: the target must hold within each representative-day group. Case-sensitive. |
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Target_OP_Flag` | Enforces the target in the operation model. |
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Target_Start_Year` | First study year in which the target applies. |
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Global_Target_Value` | Target as a fraction of energy (for example `0.8`). |
| `Clean_Energy_Generation_Penalty` | Price of a shortfall when the target is soft. |

The `Daygroup` type is stricter than `Annual`: clean generation must meet the target in every representative-day group, so it removes the flexibility to over-deliver in some periods and under-deliver in others.

**What changes in the results.** Clean-resource capacity and generation rise, and the year of first enforcement (`Clean_Energy_Generation_Target_Start_Year`) marks when the mix begins to shift. Any shortfall and its penalty cost are reported in the cost outputs.

### `CERT`

The carbon emission reduction target limits emissions relative to a reference level.

**Switching it on.** In `Simulation Configuration`:

- `Carbon_Emission_Reduction_Target_Flag` enables the target.
- `Carbon_Emission_Reduction_Global_Target_Value` is the required reduction as a fraction of the reference (for example `0.8` for an 80 percent reduction).
- `Carbon_Emission_Reduction_Target_Start_Year` is the first study year in which it applies.

**Geography and reference.** Emissions are limited per policy zone, using the zone structure from the network configuration. Because the target is a reduction, each zone also carries a reference emission level from the network data; the cap in each zone is the reference multiplied by one minus the target value. The target is a hard limit with no penalty option. It is checked against the `Emission_CO2` rates (metric tonnes of CO2 per MWh) of the generators in each zone. A missing or zero reference makes the target uninformative, so check the policy-zone data when a `CERT` case has no effect.

**What changes in the results.** Emissions fall to the cap in the years after the start year, coal and gas capacity and generation decline, and clean and storage resources increase.

### How policy settings fit together

- `Simulation Configuration` turns each policy on and sets its target or penalty.
- The network configuration workbook defines the policy geography.
- `Gen Technology` defines which technologies are eligible or count toward a policy.
- `ITC` and `PTC` provide year-by-year credit values.
- The models enforce or price the resulting requirements.

## 2. Financial settings

Technology costs are not entered only as literals. The `Gen Technology` sheet says, field by field, whether a value is entered directly, taken from the `ATB Setting` mapping, taken from the `Storage Cost and Performance` table, or derived from fuel data. The two tables below supply values only for fields that reference them.

### `ATB Setting`

`ATB Setting` maps A-LEAF technology groups to a technology-cost dataset (NREL Annual Technology Baseline). Each row is identified by `ATB_Setting_ID` and `UNITGROUP` and typically has the columns `Case`, `CRP`, `Tech`, `TechDetail`, `Scenario`, `ATB Year`, and `CAPEX_Scale`.

**Switching it on.** In `Simulation Configuration`, `ATB_Setting_ID` selects the mapping and `ATB_Year` selects the dataset year. For each technology, the model finds the row with the matching `UNITGROUP`.

**What it supplies.** Depending on the technology row, ATB can provide capital cost, fixed O&M, variable O&M, and the fixed charge rate or capital recovery factor.

!!! note "A mapping, not a blanket replacement"
    ATB values are used only for the fields that the `Gen Technology` row marks as `ATB`. Other fields keep their literal values or their other references, such as the storage table below.

### Storage cost and performance

The `Storage Cost and Performance` sheet holds cost and performance assumptions for storage technologies. `ESGC_Setting_ID` in `Simulation Configuration` selects the scenario (for example `Low_Price_Fast_Learning`), and rows are matched to technologies by `UNITGROUP`. The label `ESGC` is simply the name of this table's selector.

It supplies, for both technology-level and plant-level storage assumptions:

- round-trip efficiency (column `RTE`, used as `BATEFF`);
- annual energy throughput (`AET`), the storage technology's yearly cycling limit;
- storage capital cost;
- fixed and variable O&M.

These values affect more than investment cost:

- round-trip efficiency shapes state-of-charge transitions and how storage can back reserves;
- `AET` limits annual cycling when `Energy_Storage_AET_Limit_Flag` is on. See [Storage Modeling Reference](../database/Storage_Modeling_Reference.md#annual-energy-throughput).

A storage technology can therefore take some values from ATB, some from this table, and some as literals, as set on its `Gen Technology` row. For the full storage data path see [Storage Modeling Reference](../database/Storage_Modeling_Reference.md).

## Checking policy and financial settings for a case

Inspect the inputs in this order:

1. Case-level switches in `Simulation Configuration`.
2. Network configuration, for policy geography.
3. Technology eligibility and reference fields in `Gen Technology`.
4. Value tables: `ITC`, `PTC`, `ATB Setting`, `Storage Cost and Performance`.

If results look unexpected, check for these common causes:

- the case-level policy flag is off;
- the technology is not flagged eligible in `Gen Technology`;
- the wrong `ATB_Setting_ID` or `ESGC_Setting_ID` is selected;
- a `UNITGROUP` name differs between sheets;
- the policy zones are not at the intended geography;
- a soft target (with a penalty) is being met by paying the penalty; review the penalty cost in the outputs.
